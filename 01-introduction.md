# Root & Claw — Introduction

## Core Concepts

### Accessibility
- The foundation of TO is very simple, lowering the barrier to entry
- New players can be taught easily and quickly
- Depth and complexity are added gradually, in small portions

### Speed and Decisiveness
- No checks, calculations, or "accounting" that doesn't push the story forward
- Quick results are obtained
- In combat, heroes usually face mortal danger; rules aim to reduce conflicts to their climax

### Risk and Consequences
- TO gives heroes a significantly higher chance of success: for a price, you can take a special effort and re-roll dice
- This mechanic forces players to constantly compare risks and potential benefits
- Especially suitable for survival-oriented games

### Wounds
- Injuries that can penetrate armor
- Different variants: only wounds, characteristic reduction, states

### Edges
- Special rules, comments, and advice for running games
- Found throughout the document as sidebars

## Key Game Concepts

### Everything for the Story
- RPGs are an excellent way to create stories you'll remember for years
- TO allows creating dramatic moments that move the story forward or twist it in unexpected directions

### Player Focus
- In TO-based games, the stories always center on player characters, not the gamemaster
- Players are always the protagonists
- Rules focus on heroes and their actions, while the gamemaster manages their characters quickly and efficiently
- System designed to always provide players with meaningful choices

### Flexibility
- TO is designed to be easily adapted to various game worlds, themes, and play styles
- Using skills and advantages as modules, you can create blocks that are easily added, removed, and rearranged

## Dice and Tools

### Dice Variants
- **Classic variant**: 10-15 d6 (six-sided dice)
- **Modern variant**: Dice with different numbers of faces (d6, d8, d10, d12)
- Some rule versions use dice of different colors

### Time Units
- **Round**: 5-10 seconds (in combat)
- **Period**: 5-10 minutes (when exploring)
- **Shift**: 5-10 hours (during travel)
- **Shift types**: morning, day, evening, night

### Safety
- All participants determine the course of the story
- Responsibility comes with this
- Players should feel safe
- Discuss which topics players would prefer to avoid before starting
- Respect if someone wants to leave the game

## Character Creation - 7 Steps

1. **Choose an archetype** - Who your character is, their past, and role in the group
2. **Determine characteristic values** - Four characteristics: Body, Dexterity, Wits, Empathy
3. **Determine skill values** - 12 basic skills linked to characteristics
4. **Choose initial specializations** - Unique abilities and tricks for the character
5. **Choose personality edges** - Pride, Weakness, Dark Secret, Dream, Relationships, Best Friend
6. **Determine starting equipment** - Gear appropriate for the archetype
7. **Choose a name** for the character

## Characteristics

### Four Characteristics
- **Body**: Combination of gross physical strength and endurance
- **Dexterity**: Coordination of movements, speed, and body motor skills
- **Wits**: Intelligence, perception, and common sense
- **Empathy**: Charisma and ability to manipulate others

### Classic Variant
- Values range from 1 to 5
- Players typically distribute 14 development points among characteristics
- Minimum 2, maximum 5 points per characteristic
- Each archetype has a key characteristic where 5 points can be invested; others cannot exceed 4

### Modern Variant
- Characteristics ranked from A to D
- Each rank corresponds to a die with specific faces
- Initially all four characteristics have rank C
- 3 steps to raise ranks during character creation
- Can lower one characteristic from C to D to gain an additional step

### Health and Resolve
- **Health**: Determines how much damage a character can take before being taken out
- **Resolve**: Determines how much stress a character can take before being taken out

### Classic Variant
- Health: Average of Body and Dexterity, rounded up + 1
- Resolve: Average of Wits and Empathy, rounded up + 1

### Modern Variant
- Health: Sum of Body and Dexterity maximum dice, divided by 4, rounded up
- Resolve: Sum of Wits and Empathy maximum dice, divided by 4, rounded up

## Skills

### 12 Basic Skills (Classic)
1. **Skelm** (Body) - Lifting, moving, or breaking heavy/tough obstacles
2. **Near Combat** (Body) - Attacking or defending with cold weapons or unarmed
3. **Endurance** (Body) - Stamina, e.g., holding breath under water, resisting poison
4. **Ranged** (Dexterity) - Shooting with ranged weapons
5. **Dexterity** (Dexterity) - Risky movements and precise actions
6. **Stealth** (Dexterity) - Hiding unnoticed, stealing
7. **Craft** (Wits) - Repairing damaged items, creating new ones
8. **Observation** (Wits) - Studying terrain, gaining useful information, noticing things
9. **Survival** (Wits) - Surviving extreme temperatures, acid rain, sandstorms, etc.
10. **Healing** (Empathy) - Healing injured characters, saving lives
11. **Insight** (Empathy) - Understanding others, detecting lies
12. **Persuasion** (Empathy) - Hypnotizing, convincing others

### Modern Variant
- Skills have ranks A to D, each corresponding to a die
- Initially choose 1 skill at rank B, 2 at rank C, 3 at rank D
- Usually rank B goes to an archetype skill, others can be any

### Skill Levels (Classic)
- Level 0 to 5 (higher is better)
- Even at level 0, a hero can attempt a check using only the characteristic value and appropriate equipment

### Specializations
- Abilities, techniques, and tricks that give the hero a small advantage
- More narrow application than skills
- Usually choose one during character creation; can gain more during play

## Personality Edges

### Pride
- Once per gaming encounter, can use to gain 1 automatic success on a check
- Must explain how pride helps the hero

### Weakness
- Achilles' heel that can drag the hero into trouble
- Adds depth and individuality
- Revealing a weakness in game earns extra experience at end of encounter

### Dark Secret
- Event the character experienced before the game started
- Tool for the gamemaster to create stories
- Can affect experience earned at end of encounter

### Dream
- Main long-term goal of the character
- Earn experience by risking or sacrificing something significant in pursuit

### Relationships
- Short phrase defining character's relationship to other heroes
- Helps gamemaster create interesting challenges

### Best Friend
- Another player's character with whom your hero feels closest
- Earn experience by risking or sacrificing something significant for your best friend

## Starting Equipment

- Record all items your character carries in the "Equipment" section of the character sheet
- Items for carrying other gear and clothing don't count toward encumbrance
- Usually starting equipment depends on archetype

## Encumbrance

### Basic Load
- Can comfortably carry items equal to maximum encumbrance
- Classic: double the Body value
- Modern: maximum die of Body characteristic

### Heavy and Light Items
- Heavy items count as 2 or 3 (or more) regular items
- Light items count as half or quarter of a regular item

### Backpack
- Worn backpack allows carrying twice as many items
- but imposes -2 modifier to Dexterity checks

### Small Items
- Can be hidden in the fist; weight can be neglected
- Must still be recorded on the character sheet

### Mount Animals and Vehicles
- Mount animals can carry gear equal to their maximum load
- Or double if being led (not ridden)
- Vehicles can carry even more

### Expendables
- Category of items: food, water, ammunition, arrows, torches, air, batteries, etc.
- Two main tracking methods:
  - **Accounting and Control**: Record quantity on character sheet; each consumed item reduces it
  - **Supplies**: Each type has a supply rating; each type occupies space of one regular item
    - Periodically must make consumption checks
    - Roll dice equal to current supply, max 6
    - Each success reduces supply by 1
    - When supply reaches 0, material is gone

## Experience

### Earning Experience
- At end of each gaming encounter, discuss with the group and answer "yes" to questions:
  - Did your character participate in the encounter?
  - Did your character explore a new place?
  - Did your character defeat a dangerous opponent?
  - Did your character overcome an obstacle without using force?
  - Did your character act according to a personality edge?
  - Did your character do something outstanding?

### Spending Experience
- Between encounters or when resting
- **Skills**: Cost indicated in table; can only increase by 1 level at a time; must have used skill successfully since last advancement
- **New skill** (level 1 or rank D): 5 experience points
- **Specializations**: Always costs 10 experience points; requires a mentor who already has it; both mentor and student must spend a full shift on training; teacher must make a persuasion check at the end of the shift

## Special Efforts

When a hero fully commits to a task at risk of failure, they can make a special effort to re-roll any dice that didn't show a 1. 1s on main dice are called "curses" - they are not re-rolled.

### Price of Special Effort
- **Modern variant - Wounds and Stress**: After special effort, character takes 1 wound point (if skill linked to Body or Dexterity) or 1 stress point (if linked to Wits or Empathy) for each curse rolled
- **Characteristic Reduction**: For each curse, immediately reduce the used characteristic
- **Kubik Stressa (Stress Dice)**: After special effort, immediately gain 1 stress point per curse; these d6 dice add to skill checks (6s are successes), but if a 1 appears on a stress die, must make a panic check
- **Conditions**: After special effort, receive a condition (e.g., exhausted, annoyed, frightened); each imposes penalties on certain skill checks
- **The world answers**: for each curse on a special effort, the gamemaster may answer at once or later with a complication that fits the fiction (a ledge gives way, a witness sees too much, a tool jams). No separate currency is involved.
- **Only Once**: Special effort can only be applied once per check

## Passive Checks

- Special effort can only be performed when acting actively
- Cannot be performed when passive or unprepared (e.g., perception check to detect a hidden enemy)
- Only once per check

## Advantages and Penalties

### Classic Variant
- +1 adds 1 main die; +2 adds 2 main dice, etc.
- -1 means rolling 1 main die less, etc.
- Multiple modifiers stack

### Modern Variant
- +1 replaces one main die with a die one rank higher; +2 replaces with two ranks higher, etc.
- -1 replaces one main die with a die one rank lower, etc.
- Maximum die is d12; if only 2 d6 remain and need to lower further - remove one die
- Must always have at least 1 d6

### Complexity Modifier Table

### Advantages and Penalties (Modern)
- **Advantage**: Adds a third main die corresponding to the minimum of characteristic and skill ranks
- **Penalty**: Removes the main die with fewest faces
- Advantage and penalty cancel each other out
- Only one modifier can affect a check after cancellations

### Help
- Other players or gamemaster can help
- Must declare before rolling
- Helper must be nearby and able to influence the situation
- Each helper provides +1 modifier
- Maximum 3 helpers, so max +3
- In combat, helping costs the same type of action as the assisted character

## Extended Checks

- Sometimes one success isn't enough
- Opposed checks: must get more successes than opponent
- Each opponent's success cancels one of your successes
- Only the initiator of opposed check can make special effort, and only after opponent's check
- Can be different skills (e.g., Persuasion vs. Insight) or same skill

## Critical Failure (Idea Failure)

- If no successes are rolled on a skill check, something went wrong
- Describe why and consult gamemaster for additional consequences if needed
- Must be able to move forward - at cost of additional effort, time, or other resources
- When truly needed, one last chance: make a special effort

## Multiple Modifiers

- In classic: add/subtract main dice
- In modern: replace main dice with higher/lower ranked dice
- Can affect skill, equipment, or characteristic dice
- Always must have at least 1 d6 remaining
