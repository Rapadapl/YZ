Year Zero Engine - Master's Book

Complete reference for gamemasters running Year Zero Engine games.

Introduction

The Year Zero Engine (YZE) is a story-focused roleplaying system designed to give players meaningful choices while keeping the narrative moving forward. This master's book contains all the rules needed to run games using this system.

Character Creation

Create unique characters through a 7-step process:

  1. Choose an archetype
  2. Determine characteristic values (Body, Dexterity, Wits, Empathy)
  3. Determine skill values (12 basic skills)
  4. Choose initial specializations
  5. Choose personality edges (Pride, Weakness, Dark Secret, Dream, Relationships, Best Friend)
  6. Determine starting equipment
  7. Choose a name

Classic Variant: Characteristics 1-5, 14 points to distribute.
Modern Variant: Characteristics ranked A-D, initial rank C for all.

Combat System

Turn-based combat using initiative cards:

  • Each participant draws one card numbered 1-10 (Ace is 1)
  • Action order: 1 first, then 2, etc.
  • Each hero can take one standard action and one quick action, or two quick actions
  • Combat divided into zones (size varies)

Key Combat Rules:

  • Close combat attack: Skill - Close Combat
  • Ranged attack: Skill - Ranged, distance modifiers apply
  • Aiming: Quick action gives +2 modifier
  • Auto-fire: Additional attacks with ammunition checks
  • Cover: Provides defense value like armor

Magic System

Seven magical traditions divided between druidic and sorcerous:

Druidic Traditions (3)

  • Cleansing - Healing and protection
  • Transformation - Shapechange and elemental manipulation
  • Divination - Foreglimpse and tracking

Sorcerous Traditions (4)

  • Blood - Blood magic and life force manipulation
  • Death - Necromancy and spirit communication
  • Symbolism - Rune work and sigils
  • Elementalism - Elemental control (fire, ice, lightning)

Spell creation involves determining effect, choosing tradition, defining risk and enhancement, and establishing potential side effects.

Travel Rules

Overland travel using sector-based movement:

  • Map divided into sectors for tracking
  • Terrain type affects movement cost
  • Random encounters determined by region and terrain
  • Weather affects travel speed and survival checks
  • Resource management (food, water, fuel)

Journey tasks include navigation, resource management, and handling random encounters.

Advancement and Experience

Earn experience at the end of each gaming encounter by answering "yes" to questions:

  • Did your character participate in the encounter?
  • Did your character explore a new place?
  • Did your character defeat a dangerous opponent?
  • Did your character overcome an obstacle without using force?
  • Did your character act according to a personality edge?
  • Did your character do something outstanding?

Experience can be spent on increasing skill levels (cost table: Level 1 = 5 XP, Level 2 = 10 XP, etc.) or specializations (Rank D = 5 XP, Rank C = 10 XP, etc.).

Special Efforts

When a hero fully commits to a task at risk of failure, they can make a special effort to re-roll any dice that didn't show a 1. 1s on main dice are called "curses" and are not re-rolled.

Modern Variant Price: After special effort, character takes 1 wound point (if skill linked to Body or Dexterity) or 1 stress point (if linked to Wits or Empathy) for each curse rolled. Gamemaster receives 1 fate point.

Classic Variant: After special effort, characteristic reduction or other consequences apply.

Help Rules

Other players or the gamemaster can help by providing +1 modifier each. Maximum 3 helpers for +3 total. In combat, helping costs the same type of action as the assisted character.
