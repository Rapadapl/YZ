# Lore: the Overgrowth

The Tree grew after the people. It did not conquer the world and it did not burn it — it simply took the space that had been left, and over centuries made that space its own. The animals that lived through the coming stood up, learned to talk, and built tribes in the shadow of the trunk. What follows is what those tribes know about their world, and what they argue about around the fires.

## The Tree

The Tree is not a tree but a single living thing with no name in the old languages. Its roots run under everything the human country left behind: under the roads, under the riverbeds, under the cities. Where a root rises to the surface, the ground humps for hundreds of paces and cracks foundations. Where it goes deep, villages dig wells and pray the water does not turn out to be sap.

Old texts call it the **Worldeater**, but that word was made by people, and there are no people left. The tribes say **the Tree**, and there is more fear in that plainness than in any name.

**The heartwood.** The living centre, warm even in winter. Those who have seen it speak of amber veins that move and a low sound like breathing. The heartwood must never be burned — the first law of every Overgrowth culture — and whoever breaks it does not see morning.

**The canopy.** The upper storey: dark green, the size of a sky. On a clear day it looks like a roof; in a storm, like a sea. Seeds fall from the canopy, and after every fall a new grove is born nearby, and new mutants with it.

**The roots.** Roads for those who know how to walk them: rodents, moles, desperate couriers. Underground they form corridors that are warm, wet, and always smell of sap. The root road is the fastest way between tribes and the surest way not to come back.

**The sap.** The amber liquid the Tree pushes through its veins. It makes you drunk, heals you, mangles you, and changes the body of whoever drinks it. Medicine is boiled from sap, wells are poisoned with it, and mutations grow on it. Whoever drinks sap on purpose either grows stronger or becomes a garden.

## The rot

The rot is not a curse and not a punishment. It is how the Tree digests the living. The sickness has three stages, and the tribes know them by name.

| Stage | What happens | What is done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Blush** | Fever, green dreams, a tracery of veins rising on the palms. Not yet contagious. | Bitter root, a dry room, three days of waiting. |
| **The Bloom** | Spores come off the skin, the voice thins, the sick begin to talk about "the light under the bark". Contagious in damp air. | Isolation, burnt clothes, cut away what is affected. Healing takes −2 without clean water. |
| **The Garden** | Shoots cover the body; the person still walks and even answers, but it is no longer them. After D6 days, it stops. | No treatment. Led away from the village and left to the Tree. |

Spores live in damp: cellars, the fog of the Sapwood Marches, the breath of the Ash Choir. In a dry room with fire and salt the rot does not pass — which is why every tribe keeps at least one dry room, and why its door is always locked from the outside.

**The Ash Choir** are those who consider the Garden a mercy. They sing over the sick so they will not be afraid, and they carry spores into villages because they believe the world should be green entirely. They are not hated the way invaders are hated; they are feared the way a sickness that can talk is feared.

## Peoples

The nine lineages are not species, and not races in the old sense. They are cultures that grew out of what the ancestors were: each has its own hand-language, its own way of greeting, and its own account of what happened to the people.

| Lineage | What they call themselves | Where they live | What they are known for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raccoon-kin | **the Latch** | Stations, depots, the upper floors of ruins | Locks, wires, other people's pockets; they fix what everyone else calls rubbish. |
| Rodent-kin | **the Warren** | Tunnels, roots, cellars | They know a way under every wall and smell the rot first. |
| Hare-kin | **the Long Run** | Nowhere and everywhere | Couriers, lookouts, people who hold no land and owe no debt. |
| Mustelid-kin | **the Knot** | River bends, tight communes | Family bands, wrestling customs, a fight with no rules and no malice. |
| Bird-kin | **Ridgewatch** | Cliffs, broken towers, the canopy | They watch from above and sell warnings; a watch-call carries two valleys. |
| Reptile-kin | **the Slow Court** | Stone terraces, old courthouses | They remember debts, contracts and other people's promises; they sue where others fight. |
| Cat-kin | **the Nine Steps** | Roofs, attics, night streets | They walk without a track; their own law, their own accounts, their own night work. |
| Dog-kin | **the Pack** | Plains, roads, borders | Trackers and escorts; one is dangerous, a pack is the end of the hunt. |
| Boar-kin | **the Tusk Line** | Foothills, old tunnels | They break a line; an oath is sworn on a tusk and cannot be taken back. |

The lineages mix. There are no purebloods in the Overgrowth, and anyone who talks about "proper blood" is most likely selling sap.

## Tribes and powers

**Hollow Stair.** A city inside a dead trunk, where forty flights of stairs still stand. Neutral ground, a market, and the only place where the Latch, the Pack and the Slow Court sit at one table. A council of masters rules it, and decisions are made for as long as the pump keeps running.

**The Tusk Line.** A union of boar clans that has been slowly pushing its borders for twenty years. They do not build cities: they arrive, break the gate, leave a garrison and move on. A tusk oath binds them tighter than any crown.

**The Ash Choir.** The cult of the rot. They sing to the sick, bottle spores, and trade "mercy" to those who have lost all hope. Their songs are beautiful, and that is the worst thing that can be said about them.

**Ridgewatch.** A network of bird aeries on cliffs and towers. Not an army and not a state: they sell warnings — of a herd, a grove, the rot in a valley. The price is salt, grain, or a place on the rock.

**The Latch.** The raccoon guild that keeps the pumps, lifts and drains of Hollow Stair working. They are as influential as the city is afraid of going thirsty.

**The Slow Court.** Scaled judges and keepers of accounts. To them a debt is a physical quantity that can be passed on, pledged and collected. Their sentences are carried out by the Pack and the Tusk Line, because even boars respect someone who remembers everything.

## Lands

| Place | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Hollow Stair** | A tower-city in a dead trunk; market, dry room, council of masters. |
| **Sapwood Marches** | A swamp where sap has mixed with water. It makes you drunk, heals you and infects you; guides go first. |
| **The Long Furrow** | A ridge of root across the continent; caravans walk it and groves creep along it. |
| **Nine Wells** | A crossroads of seven roads; nine wells, of which only six give water. |
| **Chainfall** | A city under a fallen bridge; it lives on chains and trades in metal and rumour. |
| **The Splinter Shore** | A coast of shattered trunks, where the sea washes up what the Tree has dropped. |
| **The Mouth** | A canyon where the roots squeezed the river; the only passage between north and south, and the only customs house in the Overgrowth. |

## Life

**Food.** Bread from sap-flour, mushroom cheese, roasted grubs, bark broth. Meat is rare: an animal in the Overgrowth looks too much like a neighbour.

**Money.** Rings — lengths of old cable cut into equal pieces. One ring buys a bowl of broth; a hundred rings buy a working horse, if horses still exist. In Hollow Stair rings are worn on a cord, so anyone can hear how much a person owes.

**Transport.** War-boars, giant beetles under a pack, runner-birds for couriers. Machines exist, but the Latch repairs them, and only for rings.

**Weapons.** Crowbar, cleaver, root-wood spear, scrap gun (a "clatterer"), bows with bone tips. Firearms are valued, but powder does not keep long in the damp of the Overgrowth.

**Law.** Where there is a Slow Court, there is debt and contract. Where there is not, there is a tusk oath, the Pack's word, or simply whoever stood up first.

**Taboos.** Never burn heartwood. Never drink sap without a witness. Never call the rot a sickness in front of a Choir singer. Never leave a dry room open.

**Greetings.** The Latch ask, "What's broken?" The Pack say, "Walk behind me, I walked first." The Slow Court say, "The debt remembers." Ridgewatch throws down, "Wind off the canopy." The Tusk Line answers by knocking tusk against tusk — not a threat, but "I see you".

**Death.** The body is given to the Tree, except for those who died of the rot: they are burned and the ash buried in salt. The rings of the dead are divided at the funeral; if there is nothing to divide, the person lived correctly.

## Time

| Season | What happens | What the tribes do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bud** | The Tree puts out new groves; the air is sweet, the animals restless. | Gather sap, hold weddings, recruit the young. |
| **Sap** | Heat, fog, and the rot moving upriver. | Move north, into dry rooms, onto stone terraces. |
| **Husk** | The canopy sheds its leaf; days shorten, nights turn starry. | Harvest, repair, count debts, prepare for the cold. |
| **The Long Dark** | The canopy closes over the sun; snow and silence. | Sit in shelters, tell stories, eat the stores, and cut down anyone who has become a garden. |

The year is counted from the Fall of the First Leaf — a feast every people keeps in its own way, in the same week.

## Words

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **the Overgrowth** | The world after the Tree; also everything that grew after the people. |
| **Heartwood** | The living centre of the Tree; sacred and forbidden ground. |
| **Sap** | The amber liquid of the Tree; medicine, drug and source of mutations. |
| **the Rot** | The Tree's sickness; three stages — the Blush, the Bloom, the Garden. |
| **Gardeners** | Those who consider the Garden a mercy; usually the Ash Choir. |
| **Ring** | Overgrowth money; a piece of cable cut into lengths. |
| **Dry room** | A sealed room with fire and salt; the only sure refuge from spores. |
| **Root road** | The underground way along a root; fast and dangerous. |
| **Touched** | A mutant with strong bio-mutations; said with respect and caution. |
| **Choir singer** | A member of the rot cult; not an insult, but a trade people fear. |
| **Tusk oath** | A promise of the boar clans; it cannot be taken back. |
| **The debt remembers** | The Slow Court's formula: an obligation exists as long as it is remembered. |

## Truths and rumours

- They say there is a door in the heartwood, and behind it, people. Nobody has come back to confirm it.
- Choir singers do not catch the rot. It is not faith, it is a property, and the Slow Court has already set a price on the answer.
- The Tusk Line is not marching south but toward the Mouth: whoever holds the Mouth holds the trade.
- Rings that do not sink in sap have been found in Chainfall. They fetch double.
- Ridgewatch has seen a grove move against the wind. Ridgewatch has been silent about it for three seasons.
- The last human did not die of the rot. He died when he explained why the Tree came.

## Lore at the table

A lineage is a culture, not a race: it gives a skill, a trait and a way of speaking, but it never forbids being anything else. Aura is reputation made visible: a light aura opens doors with tribes and beasts, a dark one opens doors with those who have already seen your blood. Mutations are sap that stayed in the body. A psi-power is a mind that has learned to push the sap outward.

Three things are enough for a master to run the Overgrowth: the Tree is always larger than it looks; the rot is always closer than it looks; and any tribe will trade with the person it wanted to kill yesterday.
