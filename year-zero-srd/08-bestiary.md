# Bestiary

The things people meet in the Overgrowth. One page, one data source, two languages: the English
page (`bestiary.en.html`) and the Russian page (`bestiary.ru.html`) share the same list, so a
beast is written once in `assets/bestiary-data.js` and then spoken on both pages in both
languages, once per chapter, once per page.

Sixteen beasts in five chapters, plus three great things at the end. Each card shows:

- the name in the local language, and the epithet that the same beast has in the other language
- the threat dots, from ● to ●●●
- the six stat lines, one per chapter: Strength, Agility, Wits, Empathy, Health, and Armour — both
  in the classic numbered form and in the modern ranked form
- the attacks, the one thing it has against you
- the special, the thing you have to decide about, not just roll
- what it wants, and what you can loot

The three chapters are:

0. Scavengers and pests — things that want the scraps of a campfire.
1. Predators — beasts with teeth that work the same cities as people, and the same roads, and the
   same soft spot under a bridge.
2. Root and sap things — the Tree pushed into a monster's shape.
3. The rot-touched — animals and people the rot has finished, and one saint that has not quite
   finished yet.
4. Great things — things too big for a campfire and too sharp for a story.

The filter at the top of the page lets you pick a threat range, or type a keyword that matches a
name, an epithet, the special, the wants, or the loot. The count under the filter shows how many
cards are left.

How to read a beast. The threat tells roughly what it does to a careless party: a ● is an
adventure in a stretch, a ●● is a problem that takes a round of planning, a ●●● is the thing you
set up three scenes for. What it wants is the first thing to decide at the table: if you know what
it wants, you know the encounter before the first roll.

All of the stats and the special and the wants and the loot are taken from the one source data
file. If you change one line there, both pages change.
