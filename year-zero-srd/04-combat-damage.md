# Root & Claw — Combat and Damage

## Combat Basics

### Initiative

- **Using cards**: Take 10 cards numbered 1-10 (remove face cards). Number 1 is Ace.
- All players and NPCs involved in combat draw one card each; gamemaster draws for each NPC/group.
- Order of action: 1 first, then 2, etc., until all have acted.
- Place your initiative card next to your character sheet so everyone can see when your turn comes.
- Gamemaster places cards for NPCs before them.
- After all participants finish their actions in a round, the round ends and a new one begins.
- Action order is preserved - initiative is determined once at the start of the first round.

### Unexpected Attack

- If character's attack at start of combat is considered absolutely sudden by gamemaster, player can choose initiative personally.
- All other participants (including hero's allies) receive initiative cards in random order.

### Exchange Initiative

- During combat, a hero can exchange initiative with another player character or gamemaster NPC whose turn hasn't happened yet in this round.
- Must declare in their turn, before taking any actions.
- Exchanged character cannot refuse and must start their turn immediately.
- Cannot exchange with another character in this round.

### Turn Structure

- **Hero's turn**: Can take one standard action and one quick action, or two quick actions.
- **Free actions**: Don't count toward the two actions per round, but can only be taken in your turn.
  - Throwing an item on the ground or shouting a few words.
- **Standard actions**: Listed in combat tables.
- **Quick actions**: Listed in combat tables.

## Combat Movement

- During combat, hero can spend a quick action to move from current zone to adjacent zone or from medium distance to up close with opponent in same zone.
- Movement required only if character is in difficult terrain.

### Crawling Movement

- If character is prone, they can crawl.
- Crawling is a standard action; can't do it twice in a round.
- In tight zones, can only crawl.

## Close Combat

### Positioning

- If opponent is up close to character and combat-ready - cannot move away; must instead retreat.
- **Retreat**: Must make a Dexterity check. On failure, hero still retreats but opponent gets a free attack: doesn't count toward round actions and doesn't disrupt initiative order. Cannot be parried.

### Doors and Hatches

- Unlatched door or hatch: quick action to open.
- Locked door or hatch: can be broken; ordinary wooden door requires 5 damage points total. More secure doors may have defense value (see "Cover" section).

## Chase Rules

### Starting a Chase

- Gamemaster announces start of chase and initial distance (up to maximum) between participants, based on "Distance" table at start of Chapter 4.
- During chase, distance measured in categories, not zones.

### Conducting a Chase

- Chase played in rounds without initiative and normal actions.
- Each round, each participant secretly chooses one maneuver.
- Gamemaster randomly determines obstacles using tables below.
- Sequence in a round:
  1. Each participant secretly chooses maneuver.
  2. Gamemaster randomly determines obstacle and reveals it.
  3. Victim reveals maneuver first, then pursuer.

### Maneuvers

#### Outrun/Flee

- Check: Dexterity [or driving if skill exists in game]
- For each success, reduce (pursuer) or increase (victim) distance category by one.
- If after maneuvers distance exceeds maximum - chase ends.
- If after maneuvers they are up close - pursuer can immediately make close combat attack [ram vehicle].
- If distance should become less than up close and pursuer declines attack - pursuer can stay up close or overtake victim. Roles swap: pursuer becomes victim with distance equal to remaining successes (1 success = up close, 2 = near, etc.). Usually applied in races.

#### Hide

- Victim only can do this maneuver.
- Not possible at up close and with pursuer.
- At medium distance: -2 modifier; at maximum distance: +2 modifier.
- Check: opposed Stealth [vs. driving] vs. opponent's Observation.
- If successful - can end chase or make surprise attack from any distance.

#### Block Path

- Victim only.
- Check: Body [or driving]
- On success - increase distance by one category; pursuer must make Body [or driving] check or decline maneuver.

#### Intercept

- Pursuer only.
- Check: Dexterity [or driving] with -2 modifier if distance is maximum or far.
- On success - instantly up close to victim and can make close combat attack [ram vehicle].

#### Shoot/Fire

- After performing maneuver, can make ranged attack.
- If not stopping and firing, -2 modifier.
- Advanced military/police equipment with fixed weapons: no penalty.
- Some advanced vehicles have autopilot systems with "driving" skill that can shoot without spending action, replacing shooter.

### More Than Two Participants

- Simplest: divide into two groups. Only member with minimum skill value (considering maneuverability) per group performs maneuvers and checks. If several have same value, any can.
- Complex: each participant performs maneuvers independently. Must track relative distances between all participants - paper with marked distances may help.

### Specific Maneuvers

- **Outrun/Flee**: Just run full out or floor it. Each success reduces/increases distance by one category.
- **Hide**: Quick dive into alley or hide behind something. Only victim can do this. Modifiers as above. On success - end chase or make surprise attack from convenient distance.
- **Block Path**: Drop items behind to create obstacle. Only victim can do this. Check: Body [or driving]. On success - increase distance by one category; pursuer must make check or decline maneuver.
- **Intercept**: Try to cut victim off. Only pursuer can do this. Check: Dexterity [or driving] with -2 if distance is far/maximum. On success - instantly up close and can make close combat attack [ram vehicle].
- **Stop and Fire**: Stop, aim and fire. Normal ranged attack.
- **Other**: Gamemaster's discretion.

## Combat Zones

- Combat divided into zones; size can vary from few steps to 25 meters.
- Open areas have larger zones, confined spaces smaller.
- Ready-made scenarios usually have zones pre-marked; for random encounters, gamemaster can quickly sketch or just describe.

### Zone Properties

- **Narrow**: Low ceiling and/or narrow walls; can only crawl; can't move through occupied space or shoot at targets standing right behind allies.
- **Dark**: Poorly lit zone; -2 modifier to Observation checks; -2 to directional attacks; can't shoot through it.
- **Difficult**: Thick underbrush or cluttered zone; must make Dexterity check to enter; on failure enter but fall.

### Boundaries and Line of Sight

- Boundary between zones can be open or blocked by wall.
- Blocked boundary: must have door or hatch on wall; blocks line of sight even if door/hatch exists; can avoid by standing right at door and peeking through.

### Distance Categories

| Distance | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Point-blank | Right next to you |
| Close | Nearby |
| Near | A few meters from character, in same zone |
| Medium | Up to 25 meters from character, in adjacent zone |
| Far | Up to 100 meters (4 zones) from character |
| Extreme | Up to a kilometer |

## Attacks

### Close Combat Attack

- Skill: Close Combat
- If target is unaware or defenseless: +3 modifier
- If attacker is prone: -2 modifier to attacks
- If target is unaware/defenseless and prone: +3 modifier (doesn't stack with prone penalty)
- If attack hits, target takes weapon's damage value + 1 point per additional success

### Ranged Attack

- Usually skill: Ranged, but throwing weapons uses Dexterity
- Distance modifiers:
  - Point-blank: -3 (or +3 if target unaware/defenseless)
  - Close: default
  - Near: -1
  - Far: -2
  - Extreme: -3
- Large target: +2 modifier
- Small target (e.g., button, object in someone's hand): -2 modifier
- Poor lighting/darkness: -2 modifier
- Target unaware/defenseless: +3 modifier (doesn't stack with other +3 modifiers)

### Aiming

- Hero can spend a quick action to aim; gets +2 modifier to Ranged check.
- If perform any action other than shooting, or if interrupted, aim is lost.
- To aim again: spend another quick action.
- Optical sight on weapon: can spend standard action for +1 modifier (total +3); cannot shoot in same round aiming was done.

### Auto-fire

- Some weapon types can auto-fire; implementation depends on game complexity.
- **Basic version**: After hit, can immediately make another attack against same or different target. If second hit also hits, can make third, but it will definitely be the last. After all attacks, must make consumption check (ammunition).

### Ammunition Cubes (Detail Rule)

- Used if want more detail in firearm handling.
- Each weapon has "rate of fire" parameter: standard pistol = 2, automatics/ machine guns can be 5 or 6.
- During attack check, can add some ammunition dice; number must be less than or equal to rate of fire and strictly less than remaining bullets in magazine.
- If no ammunition dice added - hero spends only one bullet on attack.
- 6 on ammunition die is success, same as on main d6.
- However, ammunition success doesn't count toward hit determination; its time comes later.
- Each success on ammunition die can increase damage by 1 step or hit again same or different target in same zone.
- Each additional hit deals base weapon damage + additional successes.
- After check, sum all ammunition dice values and add 1 - this means number of bullets consumed.
- If remaining bullets in magazine are less than or equal to result - magazine is empty.

### Suppressive Fire

- If no enemies up close, hero can designate and take control of certain direction as quick action.
- From then until start of next turn, can shoot at any target in designated direction.
- Can shoot even interrupting another's claimed action.
- Example: opponent claims ranged attack intending to wait for opportunity - hero shoots first.
- Opponent cannot change their claim after hero's attack.
- Shooting at opportunity is normal attack - standard action.

### Damage

- If attack succeeds, hero hits target and deals damage equal to weapon's damage value + 1 point per additional success.
- **Armor**: May protect from damage; see Armor section.

### Armor

#### Classic Variant

- Numerical value; each success on armor check reduces damage by 1 point.

#### Modern Variant

- Rank from A to D, same as characteristics and skills (see Chapter 2).
- Each point of damage reduces armor's reliability by 1 step.
- When reliability reaches 0 - item is destroyed.

### Armor Effectiveness

- **Classic**: Roll dice equal to armor's value; each success reduces damage by 1.
- **Modern**: Roll two main dice corresponding to armor's rank; each success reduces damage by 1.

### Armor Damage

- If attack penetrates armor, its protective properties worsen - each curse on armor check reduces armor's value by 1 point.
- If armor protected from all damage, curses don't apply.
- Can be repaired with Craft skill.

### Conditions

In some TO-based games instead of damage, heroes receive **conditions**:

- **Physical**: Exhausted, battered, injured (usually 3 types)
- **Mental**: Annoyed, frightened, depressed (usually 3 types)

### Receiving Conditions

- Can receive condition from special effort (see Chapter 3).
- Each point of damage from attacks - player chooses one condition for their character.
- Instead of stress points, hero receives mental conditions; number can be reduced with Insight check.

### Condition Effects

- Each physical condition imposes -1 penalty to all skill checks related to Body and Dexterity.
- Each mental condition imposes -1 penalty to all skill checks related to Wits and Empathy.
- When character already has 3 conditions of one type and needs to receive 4th - character is **taken out** and receives corresponding injury (see below).

### Being Taken Out

- Hero can become **taken out** due to damage or stress.
- **Health and Resolve variant**: Taken out when one of these parameters reaches 0 from damage or stress.
- **Characteristic Reduction variant**: Taken out if any characteristic drops to 0.
- **Conditions variant**: Taken out when 3 conditions of same type received; then receive corresponding injury.

### Injuries

- Serious and possibly fatal character damage.
- Can be physical or mental.

### How Injuries Occur

- Physical injury when health drops to 0 (or Body characteristic to 0 in reduction variant) or when receiving certain number of physical conditions.
- Mental injury when resolve drops to 0 (or Wits characteristic to 0) or when receiving certain number of mental conditions.

### Death

- Physical injuries can be fatal.
- After receiving such injury, must make death resistance check after specified time (round, period, or shift).
- If round - check at start of next character turn.
- Death resistance using Endurance skill.
- If failed - character dies.
- If successful - survives until next period, but must check again at end.
- Stabilization: Can stabilize injured character with healing; may require medical equipment.
- **Major Death**: Some injuries kill instantly; if result is one of these - hero's story ends, even death resistance can't help.

### Healing

#### Medical Assistance

- Gamemaster or another character can help using Healing skill (standard action).
- Hero loses wounds equal to successes rolled on healing check.
- Assistance only helps **taken out** characters.

#### Natural Recovery

- If no one helps, character spontaneously loses 1 wound or stress point after one full shift.
- If character is not taken out, loses 1 point per shift.
- Character can lose wounds and stress simultaneously.
- Note: this only happens if not suffering from hunger, thirst, or cold (see "Other Dangers" Chapter 6).

#### Injured Characters

- Character can lose all wound points but still suffer from injury consequences.
- Healing injuries takes a shift plus successful Craft check; each success restores 1 point of quality/reliability.
- If fail - quality/reliability permanently drops to current value.
- If fail while quality/reliability already at 0 - item permanently breaks and cannot be repaired.

### Situational Damage

For certain damage types (e.g., fire, cold, hunger, thirst), injury tables aren't used.

- If character becomes taken out from such situational damage - refer to relevant section below.

### Fire Damage

- Modern variant: parameter "intensity" (usually 3-9) determines dice to roll; in modern variant - two dice corresponding to its rank.
- **Effect**: Each success deals 1 wound point.
- Additionally, if fire wounds character: character catches fire each round at start of turn takes damage from flame, intensity increases by 1 each round.
- If fire doesn't wound at all, it self-extinguishes.
- To extinguish flame: successful Dexterity check (standard action); blanket or similar item gives +2 modifier.

### Cold Damage

- Similar to fire; each round at start of turn character takes damage from hypothermia, intensity increases by 1 each round.
- Can protect with warm clothing or shelter.

### Falling

- Falling on hard surface automatically deals damage equal to half falling height in meters (rounded down).
- If intentionally jumping down, can make Dexterity check; each success reduces damage by 1 point.

### Overheating

- When cold around and character lacks suitable clothing or shelter, must make Endurance checks regularly.
- Cool autumn day: one check per day.
- Winter: one check per shift.
- Severe frost: one check per period.
- Additional cold protection (blanket, etc.) can give positive modifier.
- Failed check: character takes 1 damage and 1 stress point (reduces Body and Wits) and cannot recover naturally.
- If character becomes taken out from freezing - dies when next check time comes.
- Can resume natural recovery after warming, even just at regular campfire.

### Darkness

- In complete darkness, character must move by feel.
- To run in complete darkness - must make Dexterity check.
- Failure usually means taking 1 damage point.
- Can attack adjacent opponent in darkness normally, but with -2 modifier.
- Can shoot at opponent at medium distance or further - absolutely not possible.

### Drowning

- Assume all heroes can swim.
- Character in water must make Endurance checks each period to stay afloat.
- If character has metal armor - automatically sinks.
- To hold breath underwater - make Endurance check each round.
- On failure - character starts suffocating, reducing Body by 1 point (or takes 1 damage) each round until someone saves them.
- If suffocating character becomes taken out - dies after one period.

### Poisons

- Danger depends on toxicity.
- Weak poison: toxicity 3 (D in modern variant)
- Strong poison: toxicity 6 (C)
- Especially dangerous: can reach 9 (B) or more.
- After poisoning - must make opposed Endurance check vs. poison toxicity (two main dice in modern variant corresponding to rank).
- On success - effects described below; antidote can help.

### Paralyzing Poison

- Character cannot move or take actions for one period.

### Sleeping Poison

- Character falls asleep and cannot wake for one shift.

### Horseback Riding

- Horses and other mount animals can be very useful during travel: they can carry gear and in combat hero can gain advantages from sitting in saddle.

### Movement While Riding

- Can move much faster than on foot.
- Can make Dexterity check; each success allows moving one additional zone during chase.

### Close Combat While Riding

- Can only use one-handed weapons.
- Attackers targeting hero must decide: attack rider or mount.
- Attacks against rider in this case: -1 modifier.

### Ranged Attacks While Riding

- All ranged attacks from horseback: -2 modifier.
- Attackers targeting hero/rider must decide target.

### Vehicle Combat

- Controlling vehicle in combat: quick action, allows moving one zone.
- Maneuver check can allow moving more zones per success.
- Vehicle damage: same as characters.
- Vehicle has **Armor**, **Structure** (how much damage it can take before breaking), **Maneuverability** modifier for control checks.
- Critical damage: if vehicle takes damage equal to or exceeding half its Structure, random component damage (roll d6 from critical damage table).

### Cover

- Good cover can save hero's life when enemies shoot.
- To hide behind it - quick action.
- Cover has **Defense Value** working exactly like armor but only against ranged attacks.
- Cover properties:
  - Furniture: 3/D
  - Wooden door: 4/C
  - Tree trunk: 5/C
  - Wooden wall: 6/B
  - Stone wall: 8/A

### Combining Cover and Armor

- First make cover check, then armor check.
- Cover protective properties worsen same way as armor: each curse reduces value by 1.

### States

In some TO-based games instead of damage, heroes receive **states**:

- **Physical**: Exhausted, battered, injured
- **Mental**: Annoyed, frightened, depressed

### Receiving States

- Can receive from special effort (see Chapter 3).
- If check linked to Body or Dexterity - physical state; if linked to Wits or Empathy - mental state.
- Player chooses.

### State Effects

- Each physical state imposes -1 penalty to all skill checks related to Body and Dexterity.
- Each mental state imposes -1 penalty to all skill checks related to Wits and Empathy.
- When character already has 3 states of one type and needs to receive 4th - character is **taken out** and receives corresponding injury (see above).

### Death from States

- **Health and Resolve variant**: Taken out when one of these reaches 0 from damage or stress.
- **Characteristic Reduction variant**: Taken out if any characteristic drops to 0.
- **States variant**: Taken out when 3 states of same type received; then receive corresponding injury.
