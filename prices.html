    Root & Claw · Master's reference

    Prices of the Overgrowth

    What a bowl of soup, a guide, a cleaver and a coil of copper cost in rings — short enough to keep behind the screen. Treat it as a bearing, not a law: in the Overgrowth the price is set by who found whom first.

    00

    How to read a price

    A ring is a length of old cable cut into equal pieces. One ring is a bowl of soup. A hundred rings is a working horse, if horses still exist. Rings are worn on a cord, so you can hear how much debt a person carries.

    The price is the middle of a bargain. Any trader moves ±20%, and urgency, rot and war move it further. If a hero needs the thing today and the seller does not need to sell, the price doubles without a table.

    Three rules of the economy

    Nobody sells their last one. Nobody buys stolen goods in the open. And nobody gives change for a big coin — there are no big coins, only rings.

    What people carry: a beggar has D6 rings, a labourer 2D6, a master 3D6+10, a trader 6D6+30, a noble 10D6+100. Large sums almost never sit in a purse; they sit in goods — copper, salt, sap or a debt book.

    When in doubt, price a thing at two or three days of work for whoever made it. In the Overgrowth a thing is worth what a person could not make themselves.

    Master's reference. Prices are a bearing, not a rule: trade, season and need move them more than any list. Money and custom — the lore; what beasts leave — the bestiary; load and gear — the player's book.
